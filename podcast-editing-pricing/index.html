How to compare an editing quote

  1. Count the source tracks. Two separate interview tracks are generally easier to balance than a mixed video call with severe crosstalk. Specify whether a track contains one person or several.
  2. Estimate editing, not just raw minutes. Marked false starts and long pauses are within the basic pilot. Rebuilding a narrative or deleting every filler word requires much more editorial time.
  3. Ask about bad audio. Clipped words, echo or severe distortion may not be recoverable. A provider should assess a short, nonconfidential sample before committing.
  4. Confirm deliverables. Know whether WAV, MP3, stems, loudness checks, show notes, timestamps, video and publishing are included. Our pilot provides WAV/MP3, not show notes or video.
  5. Agree revision rules. One minor technical revision is included, not unlimited creative changes after the source is accepted.

When the fixed price does not fit

More than 45 minutes of raw material, three or more speaker tracks, aggressive restoration, extensive content editing, subtitles, multichannel video, music licensing and unusually short deadlines all require a separate written assessment. If it does not fit our current capacity, we will say so rather than claim a result we cannot deliver.
