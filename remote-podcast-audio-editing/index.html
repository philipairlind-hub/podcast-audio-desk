Editing podcast audio after a Riverside recording

Riverside and similar remote-recording services can capture individual speaker audio tracks. Once an interview is recorded, the remaining work may be small technical cleanup or much more involved editorial assembly. These steps are intended for audio-only spoken-word shows and apply to other multitrack remote recorders too. Podcast Audio Desk is independent and not affiliated with Riverside.

1. Export the source files, not only the final video

  • Export separate original audio for each speaker if your recording service provides it, preferably at the highest original quality available.
  • Keep a copy of the raw recording and note its format, sample rate and duration. Avoid repeated lossy conversion before editing.
  • Label files by episode and speaker. Keep a reference mix if supplied; it can make timing and speaker identification easier.
  • Provide timestamps for approved content cuts. If your show has a clear editor's cut, note which version contains the final storytelling decisions.

2. Check sync and repair limitations

Before processing, check that each voice starts at the expected position and that all participants stay aligned. Listen for abrupt jumps, missing phrases, heavy crosstalk, clipped speech and inconsistent microphone levels. Noise reduction and dynamics can improve a usable recording but cannot promise to restore words that were never captured.

3. Balance and master with restraint

Use small EQ moves, dynamics and loudness checks to make the hosts and guest comfortable to listen to together. Check introductions, guest changes and the ending by ear. Excessive noise reduction can create metallic artifacts; a stable natural voice is usually preferable to a perfectly silent background.

4. Keep editorial cuts separate from audio finishing

In a weekly interview show, a host may already have cut the conversation for content. That is a good handoff for an audio-only finisher. Our fixed-scope pilot is for cleanup and finishing of an accepted edit, with basic marked trims — not writing the episode, preparing social clips, producing video, creating captions or publishing to podcast platforms.

5. Prepare a clear remote handoff

  1. Provide the approved interview and any separate speaker tracks, plus the target published runtime.
  2. Give a timestamped short list of cuts, intro/outro positions and any music you have rights to use.
  3. Set the requested final file format and deadline, and identify one person for the revision round.
  4. Use an agreed private file-sharing method. A brief can be discussed without transmitting confidential audio.
