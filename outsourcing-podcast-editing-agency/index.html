Agency workflow / Practical guide

How podcast agencies can outsource audio finishing safely

Small podcast agencies can lose time to regular dialogue cleanup even when clients are happy with the content. A narrow remote finishing partner may help—but only if the handoff, rights and quality expectations are explicit.

Agency workflowAUDIO ONLY

Define the outside editor's responsibility

Make an exact list of what the contractor changes: noise cleanup, vocal EQ, level matching, specified cuts, WAV/MP3 delivery and the bounded revision. Keep client strategy, creative approval and other production services with the agency unless otherwise agreed.

Start with a paid, low-risk reference episode

Choose one episode whose recording format is typical and share a nonconfidential short sample for scope assessment. Complete a paid test before committing to a weekly throughput promise.

Set the data and confidentiality boundary

Agree who can access source audio, where files may be transferred, what may be stored, how long files are retained and what may be shared as portfolio material. No client files should be put into an unapproved public demo.

WORKED EXAMPLE · ILLUSTRATIVE
Agency → approved cut + two speaker tracks
Editor → cleanup, level match, WAV/MP3
Agency → final approval + customer delivery

No public case study or client disclosure without permission.

Work asynchronously when useful

Clear written briefs and a stable transfer process can be more effective than recurring meetings. Confirm turnaround and revision times in the client's timezone and allow for file availability before deadlines.

Honest commercial scope

Our $99 single-episode pilot is capped at 45 raw minutes/two tracks; the $399 manually agreed four-episode plan covers similarly scoped audio. White-label work requires a written agreement; no guaranteed 24/7 response or unlimited capacity is claimed.
