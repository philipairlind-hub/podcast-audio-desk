WAV or MP3 for podcast delivery? Save both for the right reason

Export format is the last part of the edit, but it can determine whether the handoff is easy to review, archive and publish. A source WAV and a publishable MP3 serve different purposes.

WAV / MP3
Audio editingAUDIO ONLY

WAV for a high-quality handoff

WAV typically carries uncompressed PCM audio and is useful as an editing archive or source for future mastering. It is larger, so the recipient should specify sample rate and word length rather than guessing.

MP3 for convenient preview and distribution

MP3 is a lossy compressed audio format commonly used for listening and many podcast publication workflows. The platform or publisher may have specific requirements; follow those instead of relying on a universal bit rate.

Check consistency before exporting

Verify channel layout, start and end trims, loudness, maximum peaks and deliberate music fades. Listen again to the MP3 because encoding can reveal problems not obvious during editing.

Avoid unnecessary conversions

Repeated conversions from MP3 to WAV and back do not recover information lost in the original MP3. If you have lossless source recordings, preserve them before processing.
