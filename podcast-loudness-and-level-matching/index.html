Audio editing / Practical guide

Match podcast host and guest loudness without over-compression

If a listener has to keep reaching for the volume control every time the guest speaks, a technically valid MP3 still feels unfinished. Speaker balance comes before any single final loudness number.

Separate peak meters from perceived level

Peak level shows the highest signal samples; perceived loudness depends on how sound carries over time. Two people can have similar peaks yet feel very different in volume. Use both ears and appropriate metering.

Balance each speaker in context

Listen to quiet words and energetic passages from each voice. Gently adjust individual clips or tracks before applying a shared limiter. When one person varies sharply, an automated global gain may miss the problem.

WORKED EXAMPLE · ILLUSTRATIVE
Host (normal conversation): sounds consistent
Guest (normal conversation): sounds too quiet

Fix: treat the guest source and automate phrases.
Not fix: raise whole final MP3 until peaks distort.

Avoid flattening the emotion

Over-compression can make breaths and room noise conspicuous while removing natural conversational dynamics. A controlled recording is not meant to sound like a constant wall of volume.

Check the opening and closing

Music stings and intros can be much louder than speech. Listen through speaker-to-music transitions at normal volume, then verify delivered files for unexpected clipping and encoder effects.
