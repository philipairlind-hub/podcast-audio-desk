Audio editing / Practical guide

Podcast dialogue cleanup without robotic voices

A podcast microphone can pick up hum, room tone and plosives alongside useful speech. Good cleanup improves clarity while leaving the voice believable and the recording easy to listen to.

Listen before reaching for an effect

Solo each track and identify the actual problem. A constant low hum, brief click, harsh sibilance and distant room echo require different approaches. If the source contains clipped words, no general filter can reliably recreate them.

Try small, measurable changes

High-pass filtering can reduce unnecessary low-frequency noise in suitable voices; EQ can tame honk or harshness; dynamics can reduce level variation. Apply only what the recording needs and compare with the same perceived loudness.

  • Save an untreated reference copy.
  • Process one speaker at a time.
  • A/B the exact same sentence with effects on and off.
  • Check consonants and pauses for new artifacts.

Treat noise reduction as a trade-off

Broad denoising can remove hiss but also smear speech or leave pulsing artifacts. Use modest strength first and manually repair the most disruptive moments rather than pursuing completely silent pauses.

WORKED EXAMPLE · ILLUSTRATIVE
Problem → likely first check
steady hum → inspect source/low-frequency content
speaker too soft → compare level and mic distance
click in silence → localized edit
heavy echo → restoration may be limited, rescope if needed

Balance speakers before final loudness

A consistent overall loudness target does not fix a guest who is six decibels quieter in individual passages. Smooth the apparent balance between speakers with track-level correction before final delivery processing.

Know when professional repair exceeds a pilot

Severe echo, clipping, overlapping speech, expensive restoration and extensive narrative edits are not included by default in the $99 audio-only pilot. An honest scope review may recommend a new recording instead.
