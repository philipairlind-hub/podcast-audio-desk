Audio-only podcast editing versus video podcast production

The phrase 'podcast editing' is often used for two different jobs. A weekly audio show needs clear dialogue and a good listening experience; a video show also needs camera, picture and publishing decisions.

Prices & scopeAUDIO ONLY

Audio-only work

A spoken-word audio editor typically treats the supplied recording, trims agreed material, balances voices and delivers final WAV/MP3. The number and quality of speaker tracks determine much of the difficulty.

Video podcast editing

Camera switching, framing, color, video sync, subtitles, motion graphics, B-roll and short-form excerpts require separate source files and extra production time. Even if sound is involved, these should be priced and specified independently.

WORKED EXAMPLE · ILLUSTRATIVE
Audio-only: two dialogue tracks → one finished stereo master.
Video: cameras + dialogue + visual cuts + titles + exports.
Social: selected clips + vertical reframing + captions.

Three different deliverable sets.

Decide which problem you actually have

If listeners complain about uneven microphone levels, start with an audio post-production pass. If viewers complain about jump cuts or unappealing framing, a video editor is the correct specialist. Avoid buying a larger package by default.

Agree milestones and export formats

For audio delivery, define WAV/MP3, target release date and revision scope. For video, add resolution, aspect ratios, caption requirements, social cut count and export codecs. This prevents misunderstandings about 'everything included'.

Our boundary is explicit

Podcast Audio Desk's public $99 pilot and $399 four-episode arrangement are audio-only. Video editing and social clips are not included. We can assess whether the audio finishing portion fits even when a client uses video elsewhere.
