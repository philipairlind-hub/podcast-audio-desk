Interview audio editing / Practical guide

Why separate podcast speaker tracks improve the edit

The technical benefit of recording each speaker separately is control. You can correct one noisy room or quiet guest without applying the same processing to every voice at once.

What counts as a separate speaker track?

Ideally, each person has an isolated audio recording aligned in time with the rest of the conversation. A left/right stereo mix of two voices is not always the same as two clean individual files.

Inspect the tracks before processing

Compare durations, identify silent gaps and verify that the host and guest remain synchronized. Listen for echo from headphones and overlapping speech, which can leak across tracks even in a nominally separate recording.

WORKED EXAMPLE · ILLUSTRATIVE
HOST.wav    [speech][pause][speech][speech]
GUEST.wav   [pause][speech][pause][speech]

Edit one voice without unnecessarily altering the other.
If both voices are baked into one mix, options are narrower.

Balance voices independently

A quiet guest can be leveled without boosting the host's background noise. EQ changes for the host need not be imposed on the guest. Natural voice differences should remain; the aim is comfortable listening.

Avoid accidental timing drift

Do not delete sections independently from only one track without checking sync. When removing a long pause or a false start, maintain the intended conversational timing across tracks.

Limit the pilot to a realistic source

The $99 Podcast Audio Desk pilot is specified for up to 45 minutes of raw material and two separate speaker tracks. Three-person sessions, unusually complex restoration or long edits are separately assessed rather than silently forced into the same quote.
